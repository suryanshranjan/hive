The cap wins over the minimum, so on a small invoice where 20% of the invoice is less than 500 cents, the fee is that 20% amount. Example: 10000 cents, 10 days late: 5 charged days give 250, raised to the 500 minimum, so the fee is 500. The cap here is 2000. Example: 10000 cents (100 dollars), 1000 days late: the uncapped fee would be far above the invoice, so the fee is the cap, 2000 cents (20 dollars). Example: 10000 cents (100 dollars), 2.5 days late: rounded down to 2 days, which is within the first 5 free days, so the fee is 0.

The first 5 days late are always free, so an invoice late by 5 days or fewer has a fee of 0, however the rounding would fall (100 cents for 3 days gives 0; 1 cent for 1 day gives 0; 1 cent for 100 days gives 0; 99 cents for 10 days gives 19, because the cap of 20% of the amount, rounded down, wins over the 500 minimum).

A fractional amount (1000.5) is used as is before the final rounding to a whole cent.
