## What it means, across Gates 30 to 35

The same founder, in the same hours, did two kinds of task on his phone.

| Task | What he did |
| --- | --- |
| **Answering situations before any build** (Gates 30, 31, 32, 35: 48 situations) | Answered every one, often in his own words. 28 of the 48 differed from the obvious default. He also confirmed 16 of 16 readings |
| **Reviewing the builders' decisions after a build** (Gates 30, 31, 35) | Found about half (Gate 30); found most but kept all (Gate 31); marked none (Gate 35) |

**The owner's rules come out reliably when he is asked about situations, before the build. They come out unreliably when he is asked to audit a list of decisions after it.** Reviewing 27 lines written by someone else is a different, harder task than saying what should happen in a concrete case, even with every fix to the review so far.

So Hive's design should turn around. **Interview first:** before building, Hive writes the 10 to 15 situations where this owner's rules are likely to matter, and he answers them in a few minutes on his phone. His answers become tests the builders must pass. The review after the build stays, short, as a safety net.

This is exactly what the gates have been doing by hand to score Hive (situations, verdicts, confirmed readings, hidden tests). The interview should be the product. **Next: Gate 36,** interview-first against a build with no owner, on a fresh area, with the interview generated by Hive itself.
